01 — Heritage

A medal earned in San Francisco. A craft kept in Guizhou.

The Panama–Pacific International Exposition opened in 1915 and drew eighteen million visitors to San Francisco. Among the exhibitors was a spirit from a small river town in Guizhou, carried across the Pacific in clay jars — and it sailed home with a gold medal.

The room was full of exhibits. Then a jar fell, and the room was full of Moutai.

— The legend of 1915, as it is still told in Maotai town

Moutai cannot simply be made anywhere. The town sits in a bend of the Chishui River, where summer heat, river water and the airborne microbes of the valley all take part in the recipe. The 1915 medal did not invent the method. It only announced it to the world.

  1. 1915 San Francisco Gold medal at the Panama–Pacific International Exposition. One jar broke open; the hall filled with aroma; the judges took note.
  2. 1951 Maotai town The town's family distilleries are brought together. The craft is standardized — never simplified.
  3. 2015 Maotai town A centenary edition marks one hundred years since the gold medal, bottled for collectors and the export market.

02 — The Craft

Nine steamings. Eight fermentations. Seven draws. Five years.

Baijiu is the most widely drunk spirit in the world and the least hurried. Moutai is made from sorghum, wheat, water and time — in a valley where the air itself does part of the work.

9 · 8 · 7

One full year per cycle. Carried out by hand, judged by nose.

01

The grain

Red sorghum from the surrounding hills — small, thick-husked and tough enough to be steamed nine times without collapsing.

02

The qu

Wheat is pressed into bricks of qu, the fermentation starter that turns starch into sugar and sugar into aroma.

03

The cycle

Nine steamings, eight fermentations, seven draws of liquor. One cycle takes a full year and is never rushed.

04

The clay

New liquor rests in earthen jars, breathing through the clay, aging for a minimum of five years before it is considered.

05

The blend

The final blend is assembled drop by drop from jars chosen by memory and taste — never by formula.

03 — Tasting

Soy, sesame, orchid — and a minute of quiet.

Moutai is a sipping spirit, not a mixing one. Serve it cool and quiet, in small cups, and give it a minute. The aroma arrives before the taste and stays after it.

Aroma

Toasted grain

Toasted sesame and soy sauce, dried orchid, a whisper of smoke and warm rice. The color of dark honey.

Palate

Silk and umami

Savory grain sweetness with a long, rounded middle. Full-bodied at 53%, yet there is no harsh alcoholic burn.

Finish

A minute later

Cool, clean and gently sweet. The aroma returns about a minute after the last sip — the signature of aged baijiu.

How to serve

Room temperature, 15–20 ml in a small porcelain cup. Sip slowly and let it sit on the tongue. Moutai is 53% alcohol and rewards patience.

Sip · never shoot

At the table

Roast duck, braised pork, aged hard cheese, dark chocolate. Or nothing at all — it is a complete drink on its own.

Best paired with food, or silence
